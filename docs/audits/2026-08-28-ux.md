# FinGuard web client — interaction and visual quality

**Date** 2026-08-28 · **Scope** `apps/web` · **Method** the running app driven at 420×900 (primary), 360×800 and 1440×900, plus a full read of `styles.css` and every screen component. No source file was changed.

Screenshots are in [`screenshots/`](./screenshots/) and referenced by name throughout.

---

## How to read this

Every finding is tagged:

- **[M] Measured** — a number, a hit test, a request count, a contrast ratio. Not a matter of opinion. You cannot disagree with these, only decide when to fix them.
- **[T] Taste** — a design judgement. You are the product owner; you are entitled to disagree, and I have said why I hold each one so you can push back on the reasoning rather than the conclusion.

The findings you gave me as already-known are cited where they support a larger point and are marked *(known)*. They are not re-litigated.

## 2 · The chat, because it is the product

### 2.1 The two-turn cost, measured

| | Turn 1 | Turn 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Input | «¿Cuánto gasté en gasolina el mes pasado?» | «Todas mis cuentas» (chip) |
| Wait | **19 736 ms** of "Pensando la consulta…" | **1 536 ms** |
| Output | *a question*: «¿En qué cuenta quieres consultarlo? Puedes elegir Cuenta corriente, Hucha, Fondo indexado o Todas mis cuentas.» | *the figure*: «159,46 € en gasolina el mes pasado, en 3 movimientos. Consultado en todas tus cuentas.» |

**21.3 s to a number, of which 19.7 s — 93 % — produced no information.**

Four facts change what the fix should be:

1. **The chip row already exists.** The suggestions area re-rendered as the four accounts automatically. The affordance is built; it is the *question* that is the problem, not how it is presented.
2. **The answer set is client-side data.** All three accounts are on screen at launch, from `/v1/account/summary`. The app spent 19.7 s of local inference to ask about something it already had.
3. **The product already defaults to all accounts elsewhere.** `¿Cuál es mi saldo?` answered across all three with no question. Same holder, same accounts, two policies.
4. **Two of the three options are impossible.** You cannot buy petrol from a Hucha or a Fondo indexado. The question offers a menu on which most items are wrong.

And the context *is* retained once given: «¿Y en supermercado?» answered directly, «Consultado en todas tus cuentas». So this is a **once-per-thread** 20-second toll, not a per-question one — which is better than it looked, and still the single largest interaction cost in the product.

### 2.2 The designed answer

**Answer first, state the scope, and make the scope the control.**

- **Default to all accounts and answer immediately**, exactly as the balance question already does. The scope clause is *already written and already rendered*: «Consultado en todas tus cuentas.» Make that clause the control — «en todas tus cuentas ▾», tappable, revealing the three accounts. The user has the number in 1.5 s and narrows afterwards. This is the right order: narrowing is a refinement, not a precondition. A wrong-scope answer that is one tap from correct beats a correct question that costs twenty seconds.
- **Never ask for a dimension whose answer set is small, client-side and already on screen.** Make that a rule, not a case. Reserve clarification for genuine ambiguity — two contacts called Marta, which the Bizum flow already handles well.
- **Never offer an impossible option.** Restrict the account chips to accounts that can hold the kind of movement asked about. Offering an index fund for a petrol question is worse than offering no choice, because it implies the app does not know what these accounts are.
- **Remember the scope, and show it.** Persist the last-used scope for the holder and pre-select its chip — but keep it *visible*, because a remembered filter the user cannot see is exactly how finding #30 happens (a rolling year and a calendar year both called "año", 3.6× apart, neither labelled). The rule worth adopting across the app: **remembered state is fine if it is visible and one tap from changeable.**
- **When you must ask, keep the subject.** Today, answering «el mes pasado» to «¿De qué periodo?» silently discarded «restaurantes» and returned a nine-row breakdown (finding #5). A clarification must carry the original question forward, and it should render chips — the period question rendered **prose only** («este mes, el mes pasado o los últimos 30 días») and forced typing, while the account question rendered chips. One of the two disambiguations got the affordance; the other did not.

### 2.3 The long conversation — 12 exchanges, what degraded

Run: 2 spending questions, a follow-up, a year breakdown with a chart, a restyle, a period clarification, a balance question, a Bizum cancelled at the confirm, an empty-result question.

| Measure | Value | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Thread height | 3862 px in a 402 px window — **9.6 screens** | grows unbounded |
| Fixed chrome | **222 of 624 px (36 %)**, including "Iniciar voz" + "Voz apagada", still there at turn 12 | never yields to the conversation |
| Tallest answer | **643 px = 1.6× the reading window**; plain answers 134–197 px | a chart answer can never be seen whole |
| Scroll on new answer | anchored to the user's question; newest answer **16 px visible, 403 px below fold** | the core loop is broken on every turn |
| Bubbles in DOM | all 24; **3 live Vega views, 424 SVG nodes** retained | no virtualisation, no chart unmounting |
| Suggestion chips | present after a breakdown; **absent** after a plain figure, after a restyle, and after the balance answer | inconsistent, and gone exactly when a user is deepest in |
| Earlier answers | fully legible — opacity 1, full ink, no fade | **good** |
| Cancelled payment | still styled `bubble theirs preview` — cream, «¿Confirmas?», amount bold | reads as pending forever |
| Exchange separators | none — 24 bubbles in one undifferentiated run, no timestamps, no grouping | the thread stops being navigable |
| Jump to latest | none | — |
| Heap | 23 MB at 12 turns | fine now; unbounded by construction |

**What degrades:** navigability, not legibility. Nothing fades or breaks; you simply cannot find anything, cannot see the answer you asked for, and cannot tell a dead payment proposal from a live one. Two of the twelve exchanges were disambiguation noise that will sit in the scrollback forever with the same weight as the answers.

### 2.4 What the chat gets right — and should be copied outward

This matters more than the defect list, because it means the patterns already exist in the codebase:

- **The empty-result answer is excellent.** «No he encontrado movimientos en suscripciones en lo que va de mes. Puedes buscar sin categoría o ampliar el periodo.» + three tappable recoveries: *Buscar sin categoría*, *Ampliar a tres meses*, *Mostrar categorías*. It states what it looked for, the window it used, and three ways forward. **This is exactly what Cuentas' «Nada con esos filtros.» should be** (#21).
- **The truncation disclosure is honest**: «Se muestran los 8 mayores de 9.» — the pattern the 50-row lists need (#7).
- **The payment preview is better than the dedicated Bizum screen's.** The chat gives a structured detail list (*Bizum a* → Marta Solis Vega, *Importe* → **20,00 €**) with the amount emphasised and tabular; the Bizum tab gives a 15 px inline sentence (#37). Its buttons are better labelled too — "Sí, enviar" / "No" versus "Confirmar" / "Cancelar". Same transaction, two designs, and the chat won.
- **The composer is state-aware**: its placeholder becomes "Responde sí o no" during a confirmation.
- **Cancellation is clean and fast**: «Vale, no he enviado nada.», buttons removed, placeholder restored.
- **Mis métricas' staleness handling** (drops the stale answer, sets `stale`, offers *Reintentar*, stamps «Actualizada a las 14:32») is the standard the other five screens do not meet.

The good design in this product is real and unevenly distributed. Propagating it is cheaper than inventing anything.

## 3 · Interaction audit by screen

**Inicio** — the best screen. Balances visible in **0 taps** at launch; account rows tappable; a chart CTA. Costs: the FAB covers "Ver todos" (#4); `unknown@unknown` (#16); three chat entry points (#39); the greeting «Hola, Laura» is hardcoded and takes the second-largest type on screen; "Gastos del mes" is a rolling 30 days (#30); the headline balance, the invested aside, the spend figure and all six bars are **inert** — the app's most important numbers lead nowhere; only 2 movements shown of 65; the six bars normalise differently here than on Finanzas, so the same data draws at different heights on the two screens.

**Cuentas** — carries a real cost. The accounts list is inert (#34); the single-account view is reachable only from Inicio; 14 chips across **three unrelated axes** (account, time window, category) rendered as identical pills with no indication which are mutually exclusive, 58 % off-screen (#6); a 22 px-tall search field that searches merchant only; 50 of 65 rows (#7); **no date grouping** across 4.6 screens of flat list and nothing sticky, so by row 50 the filters and the tally are long gone; «neto» mixes income and expense; tapping the highlighted "Cuentas" tab while scoped to one account does nothing. A 300 ms debounce applies to **chip taps** as well as typing, so every filter tap has guaranteed dead time with no pending state at all. «· sin clasificar» invites a correction the app cannot make — `categorise()` is exported and called from nowhere.

**Tarjetas** — best-implemented disclosure in the app (`aria-expanded`, inline expansion) and the only correct one. Costs: lands empty (#18); the promise/shortfall button (#7); the clamped, reassuring meter (#15); the window label is derived from the chip **synchronously** while the figures lag, so tapping "1 año" relabels 30-day numbers as a year; `Wallet.since` arrives on the wire and is thrown away in favour of the client's own chip label; "Bloqueada" is displayed with no unblock action and no explanation; raw enum identifiers leak for any unmapped status; the whole screen hardcodes euros.

**Finanzas** — the analysis screen. "Mes a mes" ignores the filter (#11); the category bars are uncomparable (#13); the empty "Mis métricas" card outranks the real content (#36); category rows are **inert**, so the user reads «Supermercado 5.581,33 € (103)» and cannot reach those 103 movements even though Cuentas has a `GROCERIES` chip that would show exactly them — **two screens hold the two halves of one question and are not connected**. `Actualizar` gives no feedback (#20). Two unknown enum keys both render as «Otros», as two separate rows. The metric card puts **seven** controls in one wrapping row of zero-padding text links; `Guardar` on a rename is not disabled, so a double tap sends a stale `If-Match` and produces a 412 reported with a message that asserts a cause it never verified. `Eliminar` uses `window.confirm` — a native modal that renders outside the device frame, and the only destructive confirmation in an app that builds beautiful preview cards elsewhere. Reordering waits for a full round trip before the row moves. The whole tab is expenses-only: there is no way to see income, or what is left.

**Bizum** — 4 taps + typing to send, competitive. The amount field is the app's one well-sized input (46 px, `inputMode="decimal"`, autofocused) and the button states the whole transaction («Enviar 20,00 € a Marta Ruiz Gomez»), which is the right pattern. Costs: the silent confirm failure (#1); recipient selection has no `aria-pressed` (#9); the parse hint «Escríbelo en euros y céntimos, por ejemplo 12,50.» is well written but not associated with the input (no `aria-describedby`, no `aria-invalid`), so a screen-reader user gets a silently disabled button and no reason; the preview replaces the entire form (#37); no concept field though the model supports one; no contact search; success is a silent screen swap that drops focus to `<body>`; and the balance refresh it triggers can fail silently, leaving a stale balance presented as current.

**Traspaso** — 5 taps, behind a button whose dashed `--line` border is **1.14:1** against its background, i.e. effectively invisible. No back affordance of any kind (#29). Everything in #1 applies. Two further defects: the source account is identified by **matching a balance** rather than an id, so two accounts holding the same amount (0,00 € being the obvious case) resolve to the wrong one and the confirmation screen names the wrong account; and the destination's currency is submitted for an amount typed in a field labelled `€`. The overdraft preview *(known)* is offered even though the screen holds both the balance and the amount and could refuse locally.

**Voice** — the state machine is sound: «Voz apagada» → «Conectando…» with a *Cancelar conexión* → failure. The failure copy is not: «Voz no disponible» plus the raw English «Failed to fetch» *(known)*, one line vague and one line technical, neither actionable.

## 4 · Visual audit

**Typography** is the largest undecided thing in the product. No webfont, the 2015 Bootstrap system stack (#25), 24 sizes with 18 crammed into an 8 px band (#24), four line-heights in 1576 lines, letter-spacing applied inversely to size at both ends, and ten declarations of `font-weight: 550`/`650` that a static system font snaps to 500/700 — precision that costs nothing and buys nothing. A financial product's type is its voice, and right now the app has whatever voice the operating system has. **Credit where due:** 17 selectors correctly carry `tabular-nums` across every money and count surface. That is real discipline, which is why the two gaps look like oversights — `.chart-kpi span`, the largest money figure inside a chat answer, and `.limit`, the percentage captioning a progress bar, both set money in proportional digits.

**Spacing** has no base unit. 24 distinct values, **16 of them not multiples of 4 px**, forming a continuum in 0.8 px steps from 1.6 px to 17.6 px. Card padding alone is 11.2, 12, 13.6, 14.4, 16 and 20 px depending on the card. Twelve distinct border-radii where a token exists and is bypassed, and the concentric nesting is wrong where it shows: `.card` is 20 px outer with 16 px padding (ideal inner ≈4 px) containing a 16 px inner radius, so the inner corner visibly floats. Four raw `px` declarations sit in a pasted-in block that contradicts the file's own opening comment.

**Colour** is 16 of 20 non-chart values inside a **20° hue band**. Every neutral, every gradient stop, the backdrop, the bezel and the "grey" blocked card are the brand teal at different lightness. There is no accent — `--accent` is a byte copy of `--brand-bright` (#42). `--in` (money arriving) and `--brand-bright` (this is a link) are ΔE 13 apart and read as the same green. `--surface` and `--card` are **1.09:1**, and four components are defined *entirely* by that fill — including the assistant's chat bubble, which therefore has no visible boundary. `--line` at 1.24:1 makes every divider in the app invisible (#27). And the one genuinely well-made thing — a six-slot chart palette with a written method, real hue spread and a verified 3:1 floor — **is never used by the stylesheet at all.**

**Hierarchy and density.** Uniform cards, one shadow at one strength for everything, and no mid-tones in the neutral ramp (a 51-point hole between l39 and l90) so there is nothing to build a disabled surface or a second-level divider from — which is why `.primary:disabled` renders as a 1.14:1 shape that reads as a *missing* button rather than a disabled one. The alignment axis breaks on Finanzas, where the hero stat is the only centred block in an otherwise left-aligned app. **Zero `:hover` rules in 1576 lines**, while `cursor: pointer` is declared 20 times — a promise nothing keeps on the very form factor the `.stage` backdrop exists to serve. `:active` fires a `scale(0.94)` with no transition: a single-frame snap.

**Dark mode: none, and none partially.** Zero of 18 tokens are redefined; there is no `prefers-color-scheme` block, no `[data-theme]`, no `color-scheme`. The app is dark-*shaped* by accident — `body` is `#0b1512` and the stage is a dark radial — while everything inside the phone stays white. A user on a dark-mode OS gets a full-brightness white screen floating on a near-black desk, and four inputs that set a light background without setting `color` risk light-on-light text (#28).

**What reads as decided:** the chart palette and its stated method; the `tabular-nums` policy; `max(Xrem, env(safe-area-inset-*))` applied consistently in three places; the IBAN treatment (monospace, loosened tracking, `word-break`, with the reason written down — recognising 24 characters is that step's whole job); the explicit `z-index` layering with the `elementFromPoint` bug documented next to it; the `···` placeholder that refuses to show a zero balance while still loading. Someone thought carefully in these places and wrote down why.

**What reads as templated:** the iPhone bezel, the dark radial hero gradient, five teal-to-darker-teal gradients at five different angles (two of them on objects 6.4 px tall, where a gradient cannot be seen), `--radius`/`--shadow` as a two-token "system" bypassed by ten radii and three shadows, the `.voice-*` block that shares no convention with the other 1572 lines, and `.brand .build` reaching for `--muted` and `--surface-2` — two token names from a design system that is not this one.

## 5 · Design direction

**What this app should feel like.** FinGuard's job is not to display finances; it is to end a small moment of uncertainty. Someone opens it because they want to know whether the money is there, where it went, or whether they can send twenty euros. The feeling to aim for is **calm certainty** — the app should behave like a good teller: it answers the question asked, it says what it counted, and it never makes you doubt the number. Everything about the product should optimise for the two seconds between asking and knowing. That implies a very particular aesthetic: one number per screen with its scope written beside it, generous quiet space around figures rather than uniform card padding everywhere, colour reserved for genuine exceptions, and type that reads as considered because the numbers are the design. The app is at its best when it says «159,46 € en gasolina el mes pasado, en 3 movimientos. Consultado en todas tus cuentas.» — that sentence is the product. It names the figure, the scope and the count in one breath.

**What is holding it back, first: the app does not finish its sentences.** Nearly every screen has a good idea with an unfinished edge. The FAB floats over the content it was meant to complement. The filter row hides 58 % of itself. The button that says "143" gives you 50. The chart's title is clipped mid-glyph. The answer you asked for lands below the fold. The month you are living in is drawn as a 3 px collapse. None of these are hard problems and none required a design decision — they required someone using the app for ten minutes with intent. This is where "pleasure of using it" actually lives: felt quality is mostly the *absence* of small betrayals, and this app currently has about thirty of them. Fixing this class costs little and changes the character of the product more than any new screen would.

**Second: the icon and type layers are borrowed, so the app has no face.** Twenty-six unrelated glyphs — eighteen colour emoji and eight typographic marks, mixed inside a single five-item tab bar — plus a system font stack means the product's entire visual identity is delegated to the operating system, and literally changes appearance between iOS, Android and Windows. The colour system compounds it: sixteen of twenty values in a 20° hue band, so there is no accent to direct attention with, and the one well-designed palette in the codebase is never used by the stylesheet. This is not a bug list; it is a decision nobody has made yet. A financial app can be quiet and still be unmistakable — but only if someone chooses the typeface, the icon set and the one colour that means "look here", and then holds the line.

**Third: the good design is unevenly distributed, and that is the cheapest thing to fix.** The chat's empty-result answer is genuinely excellent — it states what it looked for, the window it used, and three tappable ways forward. Its truncation disclosure is honest. Its payment preview is *better* than the dedicated payment screen's. Mis métricas has proper staleness handling, an absolute timestamp and a retry. And then five screens have no loading state at all, the payment screens cannot render their own errors, and Cuentas' empty state is four curt words above two hundred pixels of nothing. **The team already knows how to do this work.** The highest-yield move is not invention, it is propagation: take the patterns that exist and apply them everywhere the same situation arises. An empty state is always "what I looked for, the window I used, one tap out". A truncated list always says "50 de 143". A destructive action always gets the preview card, never `window.confirm`.

**And a note on where the ambition should go.** The chat is the differentiator and it is currently a good engine behind a poor window: a 402 px reading area inside a sheet that spends 36 % of itself on chrome, with a voice button as its most prominent element, a scroll position that shows you your own question instead of the reply, and chart answers 1.6× taller than the space available to read them. Before adding capability, give the answers room and put them where the eye is. The single most valuable structural change in the product would be to treat a chat answer as a **first-class object** — a card that can be seen whole, collapsed, saved, and re-opened from a screen — rather than a bubble in a scrollback. That is also the bridge to the biggest missing link in the app: Finanzas shows «Supermercado 5.581,33 € (103)» and cannot take you to those 103 movements, while Cuentas has the exact filter that would. Two screens hold the two halves of one question. Connect them, and the app starts to feel like one product instead of five tabs and a chat.

## 6 · Recommendations, ranked by felt improvement per unit of work

**Tier 1 — hours of work, changes the feel immediately**

1. **Scroll the thread to the answer, not the question.** Anchor to the top of the *reply*; for replies taller than the window, land at the top of the reply and add a "jump to latest". Fixes #2 — the core loop's most visible failure, on every turn.
2. **Render the payment error where the preview is.** Move the error render site above the early return in `Bizum.tsx` and `Transfer.tsx`, style `.confirm button:disabled`, and put a `role="status"` on the settled state. Fixes #1 — highest-severity, ~10 lines.
3. **Reserve space for the FAB.** Bottom padding on every scroll container equal to the FAB's footprint. Fixes #4 across five screens, two lines.
4. **Stop lying about list length.** Adopt the chat's own «Se muestran los N mayores de M» on Cuentas and the card charge lists, and add "cargar más". Fixes #7.
5. **Restore the search field's focus ring, and fix `theme-color` + `color-scheme: light`.** Fixes #26 and #28. Three lines, and #28 is visible on every real phone at the very top of the screen.
6. **Give the two 17 px text buttons a real hit area**, and set a `min-height` on `.chip`, `.tab` and `.confirm button`. Fixes #8.
7. **Mark the current month as incomplete** on both month strips — a hatched bar, a lighter fill, a "en curso" label. Fixes #14, and it stops the app telling users their spending collapsed.

**Tier 2 — days, and the app starts to feel considered**

8. **Kill the disambiguation turn.** Default to all accounts, make «en todas tus cuentas» the tappable scope control, never ask for client-side data, never offer impossible accounts, and carry the subject through any clarification. Fixes #3 and #5 — the largest single interaction cost in the product. (Mostly orchestrator work, which is why it is not Tier 1 despite the payoff.)
9. **Fix the two charts that cannot be read.** Scale category bars to the max on a shared track; sort by value *(known)*; make "Mes a mes" obey its filter (#11, #13).
10. **Give every screen the three states.** A skeleton with reserved height, an error with a cause and a *Reintentar*, and an empty state built like the chat's — what I looked for, the window, one tap out. Wire up the Spanish error mapper that already exists. Fixes #21, #22, and most of #12.
11. **Make the filter row honest.** Split the three axes into three labelled controls, add an edge fade and scroll-snap, add `aria-pressed`, add "quitar filtros". Fixes #6 and half of #9.
12. **Make the account rows on Cuentas do what they do on Inicio**, and connect Finanzas' category rows to Cuentas' category filter. Fixes #34 and closes the biggest missing link in the app.
13. **Cache and dedupe the fetches; preserve filters and scroll across tab switches.** Fixes #10 and #12 — this is where "feels fast" comes from.
14. **Make the sheet a real dialog**: `role="dialog"`, `aria-modal`, `inert` on the app behind, Escape to close, focus returned to the FAB. Fixes #17.

**Tier 3 — the aesthetic decisions, worth making deliberately**

15. **Choose a typeface and build a real scale.** One webfont with a distinct voice, 6–7 sizes on a stated ratio, line-heights on `body`, and letter-spacing that tightens as size grows. Fixes #24, #25 — the single largest visual lever.
16. **Choose one icon set** and delete all 26 glyphs. Fixes #23, and it is the change that most affects whether the app reads as finished.
17. **Introduce one accent that is not the brand teal**, add mid-tones to the neutral ramp, and use the chart palette that already exists. Fixes #42, #27, #35, #36.
18. **Decide about the phone frame and dark mode.** The bezel costs 15 % of the primary form factor and the fake status bar duplicates the real one (#31); dark mode is absent (#28). Both are scope calls, not bugs — but they should be *decisions*.

### If I could make only one change

**Scroll the thread to the answer instead of to the question.**

The chat is the product, and right now its central promise fails on every single turn: you ask, and you are shown your own words with a 16 px sliver of the reply. On chart answers — the ones the app works hardest to produce — the chart is *always* off-screen. It is a handful of lines, it fires on 100 % of interactions rather than only on failure, and it converts the chat from something you fight into something that answers you. Nothing else on this list changes so much for so little.

**One caveat, offered honestly:** if you weight risk over felt quality — and on a payments product that is a defensible call — swap in #2, the silent payment-confirmation failure. It fires only when the network drops, but when it does, a user taps "Confirmar" on a live payment, sees nothing at all, and taps again. That is the finding I would least want to ship.

## 7 · What I could not reach

- **A working voice session.** The voice service is a separate process on `127.0.0.1:8002` and was not running (`ERR_CONNECTION_REFUSED` on `/v1/voice/sessions`). I audited the state machine and its failure copy, but not a live capture, transcription or barge-in. Worth a second pass when that service is up.
- **A completed payment.** I deliberately blocked the confirm call rather than moving real money on a stack shared with three other agents, so I verified the failure path and the cancel path but not the settled state or the balance refresh that follows it.
- **A card in a non-`ACTIVE` state.** Both cards were `ACTIVE`, so I saw "Bloqueada" only in the code, not rendered.
- **A holder with no accounts, no cards or no contacts.** The seeded holder has three accounts, two cards and four contacts, so the true empty branches on Inicio, Tarjetas, Bizum and Traspaso were read in the source rather than driven. The `Transfer` false-negative (#21) I could reason about but not reproduce, since `summary` always resolved.
- **Real-device rendering.** Everything here is Chromium at three viewport sizes. The `theme-color` seam (#28), the double status bar (#31) and the emoji-per-platform problem (#23) are all predicted from the code and will look different — probably worse — on an actual iOS and Android handset. That is the cheapest next test.
